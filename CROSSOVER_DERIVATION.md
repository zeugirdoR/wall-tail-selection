# Boundary-Laplace and baseline definitions

Let ell(eta) be the exact log likelihood ratio, eta=mu². Approximate at the constrained MLE eta_hat by ell_hat+s(eta−eta_hat)+h(eta−eta_hat)²/2, retaining s at either boundary. Integrate this approximation against dmu/2 = d eta/(4 sqrt(eta)) on the fixed domain. In mu this is quartic near zero; around a positive interior mode the change of variables connects to the regular quadratic. This is an approximation, not a theorem of uniform accuracy or predictive superiority. The ablation instead takes the quadratic in mu, with score 2mu*s and curvature 2s+4eta*h, while preserving the same prior and likelihood maximum.

Mixture: s(0)=sum(x²−1)/2 and h(0)=−sum x⁴/6. At eta>0, with z_i=sqrt(eta)x_i, s=sum(z tanh z)/(2eta)−n/2 and h=sum[z² sech²z−z tanh z]/(4eta²), nonpositive since tanh z/z decreases. Small-z series avoids cancellation. The score need not vanish at the lower boundary. At an interior MLE it vanishes; at the upper boundary it is nonnegative.

Variance: ell=−n log(1+eta)/2+S2 eta/[2(1+eta)]. Its constrained MLE is clip(S2/n−1,0,4); s=[S2−n(1+eta)]/[2(1+eta)²] and h=[n(1+eta)−2S2]/[2(1+eta)³]. At a positive interior mode h<0, but at eta=0 it can be positive if S2<n/2. The prespecified fallback reports such cases. The exact null chi-square test is UMP for all one-sided variance alternatives; matched-alpha geometric improvements cannot exceed that ceiling. This provides a mathematically derived negative-control condition.

Drton–Plummer sBIC: equations 3.10–3.15 give, for two models/equal model priors, r²+(1−b)r−a=0 where r=L1'/L0, a=exp(ell_hat)n^(-1/4), b=exp(ell_hat)n^(-1/2). Implement the unique positive root in log scale, using rationalization when 1−b>0. These learning coefficients apply with a prior positive/smooth in mu; a uniform eta prior would change the singular coefficient. sBIC is an asymptotic fixed-point proxy, not exact evidence: https://arxiv.org/html/1309.0911 .

WBIC uses the normalized tempered posterior with beta=1/logn and score E_beta[ell] relative to M0. The log likelihood of M0 cancels because it does not depend on mu. This numerical definition implements the temperature expectation, not an RLCT surrogate or thermodynamic integration: https://jmlr.org/papers/v14/watanabe13a.html .

For both families the leading quartic null expansion is t² Z/sqrt2−t⁴/4 at mu=t n^(-1/4), but this prior local approximation is deliberately retained as a failure control. The new boundary expansion uses observed curvature rather than replacing it by its limit; no true parameter is supplied to inference. Expected test risk can use the known simulation truth only to integrate a fixed predictive distribution. We do not claim this analytical evaluation is available on real data.
